Family: [Benchmarks](../benchmarks/benchmarks.md) / viscoelastic. Status: **next target, not started.**

## The problem

Creeping flow ($\mathrm{Re} = 0$) of an Oldroyd-B fluid past a cylinder of radius $R$ centred in a channel of half-width $2R$ (2:1 blockage), $\beta = 0.59$, $\mathrm{Wi} = \lambda U/R$. The drag coefficient per unit length is

$$
K = \frac{F_x}{\eta_0 U},
$$

with $\eta_0 = \eta_s + \eta_p$. The difficulty is the thin stress boundary layer on the cylinder and the stress wake downstream, which steepen as Wi grows.

## What the references report

$K$ against Wi to four figures: Alves, Pinho and Oliveira (2001), Fan, Tanner and Phan-Thien (1999), Hulsen, Fattal and Kupferman (2005) and the log-conformation papers after it. $K = 132.36$ at $\mathrm{Wi} = 0$, falling to about 117.8 by $\mathrm{Wi} = 0.7$; the log-conformation results continue past $\mathrm{Wi} = 1$ with the wake stress not mesh-converged there.

## Our status

Not run. It needs a 2:1 channel instead of the DFG geometry and the Stokes limit of the existing cylinder driver; nothing new in the library.

## Why it is next

It is the one viscoelastic cylinder case with a tabulated number, so it is the first place the four stress histories and the two reconstructions can be ranked against a reference rather than against each other. The wall stress boundary layer that dominates the drag is exactly what the reconstruction question on the [Confined cylinder benchmark](../benchmarks/confined-cylinder-benchmark.md) is about.
