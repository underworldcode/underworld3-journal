## The problem

The DFG 2D-2 geometry (Schäfer and Turek 1996): a channel $2.2 \times 0.41$, a cylinder of diameter $D = 0.1$ centred at $(0.2, 0.2)$ (slightly off the centreline, which seeds the asymmetry), parabolic inflow of mean $U = 1$ ramped over $t = 0.5$, no slip on the walls and the cylinder, natural outflow. Forces on the cylinder give

$$
c_D = \frac{2 F_x}{\rho U^2 D}, \qquad c_L = \frac{2 F_y}{\rho U^2 D}, \qquad \mathrm{St} = \frac{f D}{U} .
$$

Viscoelastic runs use the Jeffreys element with $\beta = \eta_s/(\eta_s + \eta_p) = 0.5$, $\mathrm{Wi} = \lambda U/D$ from 0.5 to 2, Oldroyd-B and FENE-P ($L^2 = 100$), at $\mathrm{Re} = \rho U D/(\eta_s + \eta_p) = 100$ and 200.

![res 20 and res 40 meshes](../figures/cylinder-meshes.png)

## What the references report

Newtonian, Re 100: $c_D$ max 3.22–3.24, $c_L$ max 0.99–1.01, St 0.295–0.305. Viscoelastic: no reference for this geometry. Richter et al. (2010) run FENE-P past a cylinder at Re 100 in three dimensions with $\beta = 0.9$ and report that shedding weakens and St falls as Wi grows; that is a trend to compare against, not a number to match.

## Our status (2026-10-05)

| case | reference | ours |

| Re 200, Wi 0.5: lift amplitude vs resolution | — | falls with $h$ in every scheme (Eulerian 3.7, 2.9, 0.7; forward 1.7, 0.3) |

## What we claim and do not claim

We claim the Newtonian validation, the resolution collapse at Re 200, and that at Re 100, Wi 0.5 the stress reconstruction decides the regime at res 40. We do not claim which regime is right: the global projection may damp the shear-layer perturbation, or the cell fit's overshoot at the rear stagnation point (#811) may be a perturbation source. We claim no viscoelastic number for this geometry.
